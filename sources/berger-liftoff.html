Period-Overlap Note

HOME wiki is United States 1990–present. Berger’s core narrative is SpaceX 2002–2008 (Falcon 1 design, Kwajalein campaigns, first orbit, COTS/CRS rescue), framed by a 2019 Starhopper prologue and a 2008–2020 epilogue/aftermath chapter. Pre-period personal backstories (Mueller’s Idaho childhood, Koenigsmann’s German training, Cold War Kwaj missile range) are prologue only—mine lightly unless an entity page needs origin detail. No sister-period copy required.

Overview

Liftoff is Berger’s employee-driven history of SpaceX’s desperate early years: how Musk’s post-PayPal Mars impulse became a low-cost orbital rocket company; how Merlin, McGregor, and a tiny launch crew made (and remade) hardware under extreme schedule pressure; how Air Force red tape at Vandenberg forced the company to Kwajalein’s Omelek Island; and how three Falcon 1 failures nearly killed the firm before Flight Four reached orbit in September 2008 and NASA’s CRS award saved it that December. The book is less a full Musk biography than a Falcon 1 campaign chronicle—Gwynne Shotwell’s sales and NASA politics, Tom Mueller’s engines, Hans Koenigsmann’s avionics, Tim Buzza’s launches, Chris Thompson’s structures, and the young engineers who lived months on a tropical Army atoll.

For this United States 1990–present wiki the source is central to post–Shuttle commercial space policy, private launch as industrial disruption, Silicon Valley capital entering aerospace, and the 2008 dual crisis of SpaceX and Tesla. EPUB structure: Prologue; eleven numbered chapters with dated spans; Epilogue (participant updates); Acknowledgments; Key SpaceX Employees 2002–2008; Timeline; Bulent Altan’s Turkish Goulash; Index; Photo Section; About the Author; Copyright; About the Publisher.

Narrative Source Flow

Prologue

Lines 57–179. September 14, 2019: Musk at Starhopper in South Texas with his sons; FAA altitude limit that “saved” a near-crash landing; stainless-steel Mars vehicle as culmination of nearly two decades. Frame thesis: today’s Starship factory DNA is the Falcon 1 DNA—move fast, innovate, test, fly. Seeds of multiplanetary aspiration planted in the lean Falcon 1 years; path to orbit ran through a Pacific atoll where the company nearly died multiple times. Closes on Musk’s midnight smile under a full-sized Starship prototype.

Ch. 1 — Early Years

Lines 180–947. September 2000–December 2004. Opening: summer 2003 Mars close approach; Brian Bjelde’s “tour” of empty El Segundo factory becomes a meat-locker interview (hair-dye question); employee #14 after a 1 A.M. offer and a pay cut from JPL. Origin arc: Long Island Expressway talk with Adeo Ressi after PayPal ouster; NASA website with no Mars plans; Mars Oasis biosphere idea; Russian ICBM shopping that ends in price games; decision to build an own rocket. Hiring culture: Tom Mueller, Chris Thompson, Hans Koenigsmann, Phil Kassouf (North Pole compass riddle), Bulent Altan (Musk recruits via Larry Page for wife’s Google transfer). Free Coke machine as Silicon Valley transplant; schedules above urinals; Musk personally interviews every hire; “You work at SpaceX now” rebuke of Boeing/Lockheed nostalgia. By late 2005 a rocket sits on a pad half a world away.

Ch. 2 — Merlin

Lines 948–1621. August 2002–March 2003 (and engine program through early testing). Tom Mueller’s March 2003 birthday: first Merlin thrust-chamber firing at McGregor; Rémy Martin in Dixie cups; Texas trooper pulls over the white Hummer after the celebration. Mueller biography: St. Maries, Idaho logging country; geometry teacher pushes engineering; TRW years and TR-106; Reaction Research Society “BFR” amateur engine with John Garvey; dirty industrial-park meeting with Musk. Propulsion team: Tim Buzza, Jeremy Hollman; Mojave gas-generator tests (sooty cloud parks on the control tower); Bob Reagan hired Saturday evening as VP of machining—vertical integration cuts costs in half. Site search lands on Beal Aerospace’s abandoned McGregor tripod; Joe Allen hired on the spot; “cattle haul to Texas.” Foreshadow: Merlin will burn on Falcon 1 Flight One—for thirty-four seconds.

Ch. 3 — Kwaj

Lines 1622–2331. January 2003–May 2005. Opens on May 21, 2005 Vandenberg static-fire scrub: Command Van, LOX truck lost out of L.A., Goldschläger wake in Lompoc, hungover team, Musk pushing for next-day retry. Backup path: Anne Chinnery’s earlier Kwaj fact-finding; Musk cold-calls Lt. Col. Tim Mango (hung up on, then Googles, then Applebee’s ethics workaround); Greenbrier catfish dinner in Huntsville; June 2003 Thompson/Koenigsmann/Chinnery site visit. Build-out of Vandenberg pad (~$7M) then Air Force “put on ice” after successful static fire—Atlas V SLC-3 East retrofit and Titan IV / NRO billion-dollar spy sat take priority; cost-plus incumbents vs SpaceX paid only on launch. Musk’s call to Buzza: pack for Kwaj tomorrow. Army wants them; range is theirs.

Ch. 4 — Flight One

Lines 2332–3184. May 2005–June 2006. Kwaj as Army paradise: Macy’s / Kwaj Lodge mold and stamped furniture; Koenigsmann night-dives the Prinz Eugen; shipping ~30 tons of sea vans; building pad and hangar on tiny Omelek. Christmas 2005 first-stage tank implosion; February static-fire race against range maintenance blackout; Flo Li (“JLG Queen”) and Altan white-knuckling electrical lines sixty-eight feet up. March 24, 2006 launch: Merlin lights, climbs, then engine fire from fuel leak near top of main engine; rocket falls into reef east of Omelek. FalconSAT-2 Air Force Academy payload ends in ocean; Air Force watches from Colorado Springs. Salvage as scavenger hunt; Koenigsmann’s month-long silence; Musk memo praising performance and vowing “come hell or high water”; private flight home watching Team America: World Police on loop.

Ch. 5 — Selling Rockets

Lines 3185–3906. August 2002–August 2006. Gwynne Shotwell’s impromptu lunch-drop visit with Koenigsmann; ten-minute Musk meeting; weeks of dithering mid-divorce then freeway call—“I’m going to take the job.” Musk rejects written sales plans: just do. Smithsonian / Independence Avenue December 2003 hollow Falcon 1 reveal (crossing-arm ding on rail tracks; midnight tarp in freezing rain after the party). Early customers: DARPA Falcon program (~16Mtotal),MalaysianRazakSAT( 6M), Office of Force Transformation. Musk protest of NASA’s Kistler favoritism reopens COTS competition; twenty-one bids → six finalists. August 2006 factory-floor announcement: “We fucking won”—$278M COTS, path to Falcon 9/Dragon and Hawthorne move; NASA endorsement after Flight One failure.

Ch. 6 — Flight Two

Lines 3907–4732. March 2006–March 2007. Reward vacations for 200+ travel days; Hollman’s Bora Bora honeymoon during first static-fire attempt. Musk publicly blames Flight One on pad-processing error by an experienced technician; Hollman and Eddie Thomas under fire; Mueller–Musk rift. Fairing and structures work through Flo Li’s path from Delaware / Blue Planet IMAX dream to fairing lead. Slosh-baffle debate: Musk ranks risk low, fear of welding thin second-stage walls. March 2007 campaign: scrub over abort sensor, Buzza holds against Musk from 5,000 miles away; next-day chamber-pressure abort from cool recycled kerosene; half-drain retank improvisation; liftoff. First stage works; fairing separates; Earth-from-space view; then second-stage spin (~60 rpm) from LOX slosh, Kestrel flameout near Kingman Reef. Near-miss celebrated as ~95% success; legacy: always list top eleven risks; Musk’s mass/ISP war and “faster, better, cheaper—pick three” refusal.

Ch. 7 — Texas

Lines 4733–5426. January 2003–August 2008. Mueller left off Zero-G reward flight after Flight One; hug with Musk when Kestrel lights on Flight Two restores trust. Beal Aerospace cautionary tale ($200M, large engine fired, dissolved citing NASA/contractor subsidies). McGregor ranch wildness; Errol Musk visit; Secret Service curiosity from engine ruckus. Regenerative Merlin 1C vs ablative Merlin 1A/1B—Musk right to push regen early; 1B scrapped. Zach Dunn’s 2006 Stanford internship: Merlin roar hooks him; NASA money and Jim Maser CEO bring documentation culture; Friday ice-cream runs fade. Dunn full-time July 2007; Merlin 1C qualifies after >10 equivalent launch firings by February 2008—bound for Flight Three.

Ch. 8 — Flight Three

Lines 5427–6079. May 2008–August 2008. Dunn owns Flight Three propulsion months into full-time job. Cracked Kestrel niobium skirt after Hawaii courier handoff (employee watchdog absent); team flies home empty-handed. Payloads: Air Force Trailblazer, NASA CubeSats—real customers after dummy Flight Two. Kwaj life: Minuteman III reentry “fireflies” over Omelek; Peregrine Falcon boat and “Salty Dog” / “Space Mom”; Huey pilots who “only fly as high as I can jump”; Hawthorne lease at 1 Rocket Road (Reagan guts ugly Boeing building). Flight Three: regeneratively cooled Merlin residual fuel in cooling channels produces tiny post-cutoff thrust (~10 psi in vacuum)—stages collide. Preventable; software fix is +4 seconds before separation. Dark jokes about buying own tickets home; Chinnery: three failures is a lot—“Hardly anyone survives that in the aerospace world.”

Ch. 9 — Eight Weeks

Lines 6080–6658. August 2008–September 2008. Post-failure staff meeting in Von Braun conference room; Musk’s “shitty summer”—Tesla financing failed, divorce, no house, capital split with SolarCity/Tesla, recession starting. Instead of blame: build and fly the last Falcon 1 in roughly six weeks (demo mission; Malaysians won’t risk satellite). Structures team cobbles hardware; C-17 transit damages stage; on Omelek the crew strips the first stage “like a Chevy,” engine on blocks, replace baffles, pressure-test with LOX/kerosene (failure would be game over). Wrinkles smooth under pressure; stage flightworthy in days. Hard rock vs classical aerospace self-image; grilled steaks and Turkish goulash under the stars. Fly or die.

Ch. 10 — Flight Four

Lines 6659–7159. September 29, 2008 (campaign through celebration). Buzza’s midnight bike to North Point; Southern Cross calm. Dunn bikes to Bond-villain Army facility / control room. Musk stays in California—Disneyland Space Mountain with Kimbal and kids as recession superstition; VC fundraising collapses industry-wide. Launch succeeds: first privately developed liquid rocket to orbit; RatSat / second stage seen again after ~90 minutes. Hawthorne Purple Orchid cheers; Kwaj team bikes chanting “Orbit,” reunites with Peregrine Falcon fallback crew, parties at Veteran’s Hall; decoy sober riders (Salty Dog / Space Mom) distract MPs so the rest can skinny-dip in the lagoon. Hollman already leaving for family—Merlin legacy secured; child conceived weeks earlier.

Ch. 11 — Always Go to Eleven

Lines 7160–7944. September 2008–May 2020. Orbit without jubilation for Musk—cortisol, relief only, “patient survived.” Payroll >500; Founders Fund 20Mhelpslittle;Shotwellseessix–eightweekstomissedpayroll.Splitremaining 30M between SpaceX and Tesla (“two children”). CRS bid as last card; Falcon 5 idea yields to Falcon 9 when NASA wants multi-ton Dragon; nine-Merlin clustering vs Merlin 2 path not taken. November 2008 full-duration nine-engine McGregor firing. December 22, 2008: Gerstenmaier/Suffredini call—CRS win; Christmas Eve Tesla financing closes. Shotwell becomes president December 2008 (after Jim Maser experiment fails). July 2009 Malaysian commercial Falcon 1 success; then Musk kills Falcon 1/1e for Falcon 9 focus; ORBCOMM moves to Falcon 9; Omelek chipped to golf-ball concrete and reclaimed by crabs. Cape Canaveral westward recovery; industry disruption (ULA Tobey recording); Peter Beck / Rocket Lab on legitimizing New Space. Closes on Musk’s “goddamn outrage” that nineteen years on, humans are still not on Mars—and Starship as the next step.

Epilogue

Lines 7945–8562. Participant coda with Falcon 1–era titles and later paths: Chris Thompson (quit/return/raise; family cost; Virgin Galactic → Astra); Bulent Altan (avionics lead, Starlink, goulash recipe as procedure); Anne Chinnery (Vandenberg return, McGregor landing tests, Firefly; DoD attitude shift); Flo Li and others on luck and focus; Brian Bjelde (mission manager → VP HR; Omelek as company DNA filter); Hans Koenigsmann (still there; mission assurance; Musk as talent picker); further voices on sacrifice, culture, and what the early years meant. Book’s human ledger of who stayed, who left, and why the Kwaj crucible still hangs on conference-room walls.
