Period-Overlap Note

This source has been copied from United States 1946-89 into the United States 1990–present wiki because Chapters 24–33 + Appendix cover CNN under Tom Johnson and the Gulf War, Cartoon Network/TNT strategy, network merger feelers, the Time Warner combination, the UN Foundation billion-dollar pledge, the AOL deal that diluted Turner’s power, September 11 from CNN Center, and stepping away into philanthropy—core post–Cold War media-consolidation material. Chs. 1–3 childhood are background only.

Overview

Call Me Ted is Robert Edward “Ted” Turner III’s first-person life of media entrepreneurship and competitive sailing, written with Bill Burke. For this United States 1990–present wiki the usable core is the post–Berlin Wall arc: CNN’s Gulf War legitimacy, entertainment-network expansion under Time Warner constraints, the 1995–96 merger into Time Warner, the UN billion pledge, and the 2000 AOL combination that phased him out. Period-gate hard on pre-1990 founding chapters except as continuity.

EPUB structure: copyright/Contents; dedication; Preface; Chapters 1–33; Appendix (Eleven Voluntary Initiatives); Acknowledgments; photo credits.

Front matter / Contents / dedication

Lines 1–111. © 2008 Turner Works, LLC; Grand Central / Hachette imprint; ISBN 978-0-446-54336-1; Contents listing Preface through Acknowledgments; dedication to Turner’s children.

Preface

Lines 112–156. Why write now after stepping away; setbacks (father and sister, sailing near-death, Braves losing years, later wealth loss); philanthropy and five children; invitation to the ride.

Chapter 1 — Early Years

Lines 157–492. Birth 19 November 1938, Cincinnati; Ed Turner and Florence Rooney; sister Mary Jean (1941); father’s Navy service and four-year-old boarding school; postwar family moves; early nature passion and difficult father.

Chapter 2 — McCallie

Lines 493–907. Seventh-grade boarding at McCallie military academy, Chattanooga; youngest boarder; discipline, demerits, and competitive drive; father’s purse-string control of schooling.

Chapter 3 — College and the Coast Guard

Lines 908–1528. Senior-year college choice; Brown University; mischief with Peter Dames; Coast Guard service; father’s insistence on eventual takeover of the billboard company.

Chapter 4 — Billboards

Lines 1529–1913. Post-college Florida rebellion with Dames; return into Turner Advertising; learning outdoor sales and plant operations under Ed Turner’s expansion in the South.

Chapter 5 — Tragedy

Lines 1914–2094. Summer 1962 General Outdoor deal with Bob Naegele to make Turner the South’s largest billboard operator; father’s mounting stress and erratic behavior; suicide (gunshot), March 1963.

Chapter 6 — Picking Up the Pieces

Lines 2095–2320. Jimmy Brown first on the scene; warning signs and $50,000 check to stepmother Jane; holding the company together amid debt and competitor pressure.

Chapter 7 — On My Own

Lines 2321–2667. By end of March 1963 Turner as chairman/president; son Robert Edward Turner IV born May 1963; marriage to Judy failing; consolidating control of the billboard business.

Chapter 8 — Sailing Gets Serious

Lines 2668–3038. Mid-1960s sailing intensity alongside business; Flying Dutchman and yacht-club life; Ferrari anecdote; racing ladder that later leads to ocean racing and the Cup.

Chapter 9 — WTCG: “Watch This Channel Grow!”

Lines 3039–3619. Late-1960s radio holdings without Atlanta 24-hour outlet; billboard ad for UHF WJRJ Channel 17; purchase and rebranding push; movies, wrestling, counterprogramming against network affiliates.

Chapter 10 — The Braves

Lines 3620–4161. Braves telecasts as ratings and UHF-receiver catalyst (~100,000 estimated new converters); ballpark enthusiasm amid losing seasons; path toward franchise ownership to secure rights and identity.

Chapter 11 — The SuperStation

Lines 4162–4763. Growth ceiling in Atlanta vs. Big Three affiliates; satellite distribution leap—WTCG as national cable SuperStation; “bigger footprint” strategy and operator relationships.

Chapter 12 — The America’s Cup

Lines 4764–5212. Competitive sailing peak around SuperStation launch; campaign toward and winning the 1977 America’s Cup; “Captain Outrageous” public persona alongside media expansion.

Chapter 13 — Cable News Network

Lines 5213–5526. Cable partnership creed (“you run the wires, I’ll make them sing”—Bob Magness/TCI); 1978 decision to launch all-news channel himself after years of no one else doing it; naming and early planning for CNN.

Chapter 14 — Fastnet

Lines 5527–5872. 1979 Fastnet Race ordeal while CNN preparations advance; near-disaster at sea; return to intensify the nine-months-to-launch countdown.

Chapter 15 — CNN Launch

Lines 5873–6200. Country-club facility retrofit; Reese Schonfeld / Burt Reinhardt hiring surge; launching without enough capital to reach profitability; race against copycats and lenders’ skepticism.

Chapter 16 — A Dagger Pointed at Our Heart

Lines 6201–6661. 1980 America’s Cup defense attempt with Courageous vs. Dennis Conner while CNN is newborn; competitive and financial strain; “dagger” metaphor for threats to the young news network.

Chapter 17 — World Citizen

Lines 6662–6905. International travel and Cold War–era public diplomacy instincts; using media reach and sports for global visibility beyond U.S. cable homes.

Chapter 18 — The Goodwill Games

Lines 6906–7118. Post-Olympic-boycott alternative: Goodwill Games concept and Moscow staging; sports diplomacy brand alongside CNN’s international ambitions.

Chapter 19 — CBS

Lines 7119–7538. Mid-1980s scale logic—own broadcast network plus cable; exploratory pursuit of CBS; Big Three slow to enter cable; failed/blocked path that pushes other expansion routes.

Chapter 20 — MGM

Lines 7539–7903. Studio merger talks with Kirk Kerkorian / MGM-UA to secure film library against Hollywood leverage; purchase and debt load that reshape Turner’s balance sheet.

Chapter 21 — The Cable Operators

Lines 7904–8198. John Malone / TCI rescue and cable MSO equity alliance after MGM strain; shared fight against broadcast networks; accepting outside ownership to keep assets in the cable camp.

Chapter 22 — Meeting Jane Fonda

Lines 8199–8492. Courtship and marriage to Jane Fonda; public/political celebrity intersection with Turner’s media persona.

Chapter 23 — “Give Me Land, Lots of Land”

Lines 8493–8694. Montana Bar None then Flying D Ranch (~119,000 acres); bison pasture vision; land conservation turn that prefigures later philanthropy.

Chapter 24 — The Gulf War

Lines 8695–9058. Tom Johnson hired to lead CNN (1990); Gulf War coverage cementing CNN’s global news role—wiki core for this period.

Chapter 25 — Movies and Cartoons

Lines 9059–9466. 1990s strategy: own programming; TNT success; Cartoon Network and library exploitation under Time Warner board constraints.

Chapter 26 — Networking

Lines 9467–9857. Continued ABC/NBC/CBS merger feelers; Cap Cities/ABC Olympics talks; Jerry Levin blocking network purchase.

Chapter 27 — Time Warner Merger

Lines 9858–10188. Summer 1995: O.J. ratings spike; agreement to merge Turner into Time Warner.

Chapter 28 — The New Time Warner

Lines 10189–10674. Post-announcement Braves World Series 1995; integration politics inside the combined company.

Chapter 29 — Billion-Dollar Gift

Lines 10675–11023. Philanthropy seminar roots; $1 billion pledge to UN causes / UN Foundation.

Chapter 30 — Transitions

Lines 11024–11377. Jonathan Levin murder; personal and corporate transitions around the UN Foundation decision.

Chapter 31 — AOL: Phased Out and Fenced In

Lines 11378–11866. January 2000 AOL–Time Warner deal whirlwind; divorce from Jane same week; dilution and loss of influence.

Chapter 32 — Stepping Away

Lines 11867–12370. September 11 from CNN Center; final exit from corporate power.

Chapter 33 — Onward and Upward

Lines 12371–12864. Post-business focus on global problems, conservation, and energy; closing creed.

Appendix — Eleven Voluntary Initiatives

Lines 12865–12916. Pocket list of personal pledges (planet, weapons, UN, renewable energy/climate added 2008).

Acknowledgments

Lines 12917–12954. Mort Janklow; Hachette team; Debbie Masterson, Taylor Glover; Elizabeth Dewberry; Bill Burke; photo credits.
