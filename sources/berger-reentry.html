Period-Overlap Note

HOME wiki is United States 1990–present. Reentry is the sequel to Berger’s Liftoff: after Falcon 1’s early grind, this book covers the Falcon 9 reuse / Dragon / Falcon Heavy / Starlink / Starship era roughly 2008–2023. Chronology jumps (e.g. Ch. 11 opens on December 2008 Commercial Crew politics; Ch. 13 opens on Falcon Heavy 2018 while weaving earlier cadence work). Almost the entire narrative sits inside the period; no sister-period copy required.

Overview

Reentry is Berger’s behind-the-scenes history of SpaceX becoming the dominant U.S. launch company: Falcon 9 development and first orbit; Cargo Dragon’s cheese gag, C2 berthing drama, and CRS-7 loss; the push for first-stage reusability (Grasshopper, barge landings, densified propellant); Iridium deals and the AMOS-6 pad explosion; Musk’s Guadalajara Mars speech and Starlink/Starship cost logic; Commercial Crew politics and Crew Dragon (including SuperDraco explosion after Demo-1); Falcon Heavy as “stepping stone”; and Demo-2 restoring U.S. crew launch in 2020. The Prologue frames April 2023 Starship Flight 1 from South Padre; the Epilogue pivots hard to Starship and worries aloud about Musk’s Twitter/X turn.

For this United States 1990–present wiki the source is central to post-Shuttle commercial spaceflight, NASA COTS/Commercial Crew, reusable rocketry, military/commercial satellite launch competition with ULA, and the industrial politics of a private multiplanetary program. EPUB structure: Praise; title/copyright; dedication; Prologue; thirteen numbered chapters; Epilogue; Acknowledgments; photo credits/apparatus; Index.

Narrative Source Flow

Prologue

Lines 108–242. April 20, 2023, South Padre Island: full-stack Starship prototype lifts off, loses engines, self-destructs over the Gulf. Musk’s gut-twisted nerves; Super Heavy / Starship full-reuse vision versus Saturn V throwaway stages; Berger’s thesis that SpaceX succeeded because of Musk more than despite him—and a warning that Musk’s rightward politics and global conflicts may force a reckoning with NASA/DoD customers.

Ch. 1 — A Violent Beast

Lines 243–1060. November 22, 2008, McGregor: Tom Mueller runs outside the bunker to feel the first full-duration Falcon 9 nine-Merlin hot-fire. Industry skepticism (Bolden, Shelby’s “rocket hobbyists”); Musk’s airplane-analogy case for reuse; noise physics of the Texas night test; Falcon 9 as the machine that would answer the Falcon 1 record and enter the heavy-lift club.

Ch. 2 — Learning to Be Scrappy

Lines 1061–1842. January 10, 2009, Cape Canaveral: Falcon 9 raised vertical for a photo-op (“Capricorn One”) while half the stack is still fake—hollow second stage, mock fairing. “Green lights to Malibu” / Musk Time; Roger Carlson’s tower photos; Sanford-and-Son scrap culture (LOX ball bought for $86,001; interns lowered into the tank); Florida pad build-out on shoestring amid Air Force/NASA interfaces.

Ch. 3 — Flight One: Reaching Orbit

Lines 1843–2688. Opens September 28, 2008 (Falcon 1 success recruiting Robert Rose from Bend video-game work)—then chronology jumps into Falcon 9 software/avionics grind. Rose’s “Elon interview” (hybrids are “bullshit”); flight software as liability; Hans Koenigsmann’s flight-termination battery hell; boat-in-exclusion-zone scrub; TEA-TEB abort then clean countdown. First Falcon 9 reaches orbit with a Dragon boilerplate—orbit on the first try after Falcon 1’s three failures.

Ch. 4 — Flight Two: Dragon’s Debut

Lines 2689–3459. September 2010 Hawthorne: Musk’s Monty Python cheese gag—Le Brouère wheel as “super top secret” Dragon payload; Magic Dragon → Dragon naming; NASA COTS lifelines; Chris Thompson structures culture and employee-number patches. Dragon’s first orbital flight as media theater and spacecraft proof-point toward Mars settlement.

Ch. 5 — Flight Three: Dragon’s Desperate Ride

Lines 3460–4371. May 25, 2012 Houston/Hawthorne: C2 berthing—Holly Ridings as flight director holding Dragon 250 feet from ISS while sensors fail; on-the-fly software rewrite NASA normally forbids; Challenger childhood trauma informing her risk calculus. CUCU radio / Altan avionics anxiety; Star Waggon crash pads in Hawthorne parking lot; Dragon berths and SpaceX becomes a real ISS partner.

Ch. 6 — Ain’t Coming Back

Lines 4372–5155. January 2012 onward: Vandenberg polar pad buildup (Benjamin Kellie’s DDR interview lore); Great LOX Boil-Off of 2013 (frozen water in actuator; Led Zeppelin on loop); Tim Buzza’s rupture with Musk after the crane/belly-band incident—“That was the end of SpaceX for me.” Fairing cost fights (“Black Sunday”); CRS-2 close of Falcon 9 v1.0; reusability still mostly aspiration while cadence and second coasts demand scrapyard ingenuity.

Ch. 7 — Get the Barge Ready

Lines 5156–6117. April 20, 2014 Easter Sunday: Zach Dunn woken into a Joint Staff J-3 roll call to brief hunting a soft-landed first stage in Atlantic storm seas. History of propulsive landing (Delta Clipper); Grasshopper hops at McGregor; Just Read the Instructions drone ship; early ASDS tip-overs and near-misses. Sets the recovery theater that will define Falcon 9’s economic revolution.

Ch. 8 — Tragedy and Triumph

Lines 6118–7033. June 28, 2015 CRS-7: Falcon 9 breakup; David Giger’s “Dragon is alive!” and failed manual parachute sequence (“Dragon shouldn’t be fucking stupid”). Helium COPV strut failure root cause; Pad 39A lease politics; densified LOX program (NIST table calls; Phillip Rench from SeaWorld valves). December 21, 2015 return-to-flight lands first stage at Landing Zone 1—Christmas saved; reuse becomes real hardware, not PowerPoint.

Ch. 9 — F-Squared and the AMOS-6 Disaster

Lines 7034–7919. Opens February 2009 Iridium NEXT negotiation (Desch vs Musk; ~492Mvs1.2B next bid)—then SES-9 delays birth “F-squared” (“fucking flawless”). April 2016 CRS-8 / Of Course I Still Love You first drone-ship landing. September 1, 2016 AMOS-6 static-fire explosion destroys SLC-40 and the Israeli satellite; swamp debris hunt, AVIRIS jet, COPV buckling in densified LOX. Return-to-flight discipline and the scar that raises load-and-go crew-fueling stakes.

Ch. 10 — The Cost of Mars

Lines 7920–8807. September 2016 Guadalajara IAC: Musk refuses to cancel Mars keynote after AMOS-6; 100-person ships, “lamp of consciousness,” multiplanetary species. Starlink as Comcast competitor and cash engine for Starship; Tintin prototypes → sixty-sat batches → thousands by 2023. Boca Chica / Starbase industrial build; stainless Starship prototypes and early hops—Mars priced in reuse cadence and constellation revenue.

Ch. 11 — The Fabergé Egg

Lines 8808–9520. December 2008 Washington thread: Lori Garver tucks Commercial Crew seed money into the Recovery Act; Griffin skepticism; Boeing/SpaceX/Sierra Nevada competition. SuperDraco propulsive-landing pitch vs NASA/astronaut hatred of it; DragonFly abort/propulsive tests; Red Dragon Mars tease abandoned; Fabergé-egg fragility of crew systems; load-and-go fueling fights after AMOS-6; path toward awarding Crew Dragon alongside Boeing Starliner.

Ch. 12 — A Second Space Age

Lines 9521–10328. April 20, 2019: after Demo-1 success, Crew Dragon explodes during SuperDraco ground test at LZ-1—Doug Hurley’s “We lost the capsule” text. Boeing astronaut-culture contrast; chute-show desert tribe; Bridenstine “It’s time to deliver” tweet vs Starship unveiling; Demo-2 May 2020 scrub (field mills / Trump anger) then Hurley–Behnken launch restoring U.S. crew access in pandemic/George Floyd unrest backdrop—America’s second space age as private crew taxi.

Ch. 13 — Steamroller

Lines 10329–11392. February 5, 2018 LC-39A: Musk shrugs at Falcon Heavy—“I think we need to step up our game”—already eyeing Starship. Twenty-seven engines / N1 ghost; TE runaway brake scare; 39A cadence (nine-day turnarounds, Intelsat T-10 aborts); Range-as-airport culture; Blue Origin/Bob Smith competitive foil; Maezawa lunar Starship PR. Steamroller metaphor: every win moves the goalposts toward Mars.

Epilogue

Lines 11393–11852. Four days after Behnken/Hurley August 2020 splashdown, Musk company email: Starship is top priority—relocate to South Texas. Berger’s personal stake (Ars Technica, Artemis dependence on Starship); worry that Musk alone can stop the steamroller (Twitter/X purchase, credibility costs); competitor survey (Blue Origin, Rocket Lab, China, etc.); provisional close on unfinished Mars bet.

Acknowledgments / apparatus

Lines 11853–13110. Interview method: less Musk access than Liftoff (Isaacson/Twitter timing); emphasis on departed engineers (Buzza, Mueller, Dunn, Koenigsmann, et al.); BenBella cover symmetry with Liftoff; photo credits, index, and trailing extract apparatus.

Coverage Guide

Falcon 9 development and first flights

Chs. 1–3 for McGregor hot-fire, Capricorn One theater, and first orbital success.

Cargo Dragon and ISS partnership

Chs. 4–5 and 8 for cheese debut, C2 berthing, CRS-7 loss, and cargo return-to-flight.

Reusability (Grasshopper → barge → LZ-1)

Chs. 6–8 for Vandenberg/LOX culture, ASDS program, densification, and December 2015 landing.

Commercial satellite market and pad disasters

Ch. 9 for Iridium pricing power, F-squared cadence culture, CRS-8 barge landing, and AMOS-6.

Mars vision, Starlink, and Starship

Ch. 10, Prologue, Ch. 13, and Epilogue for Guadalajara speech, constellation economics, Boca Chica, and Flight 1.

Commercial Crew / Demo-2

Chs. 11–12 for Recovery Act politics, SuperDraco explosion, Bridenstine pressure, and 2020 crew return.

Leadership culture and industrial politics

Throughout; especially Prologue/Epilogue on Musk, and Chs. 2, 6, 9, 13 on scrap culture, firings, and cadence steamroller.
