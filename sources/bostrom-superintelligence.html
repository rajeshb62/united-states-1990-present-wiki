Overview

Superintelligence is Bostrom’s analytic treatment of what would happen if machine minds surpassed human general intelligence: how such systems might be built, how fast capability might escalate, why a first-mover could reshape the future, and why aligning superintelligent goals with human welfare—the control problem and value-loading problem—may be extraordinarily hard. The book opens with the sparrows-and-owl fable (build power before mastering control) and proceeds through paths, kinetics, strategic advantage, cognitive superpowers, motivation theory, default-risk arguments, control methods, multipolar economics, value acquisition, indirect normativity (including Coherent Extrapolated Volition), and a closing strategic picture plus practical recommendations.

For this United States 1990–present wiki the source is not a chronological US history narrative. It belongs on AI risk, long-horizon technology strategy, and post–Cold War tech-discourse pages—optionally as thin bridge interest for Silicon Valley / AI-culture context (expert surveys, industry actors in acknowledgments, acceleration debates). No period-overlap copy to 1866–1919, 1920–45, or 1946–89 wikis. PDF structure: front matter and sparrow fable; Preface and Acknowledgments; Contents and apparatus lists; fifteen body chapters marked C H A P TE R N; Notes; Bibliography; Index.

Narrative Source Flow

Front matter / Sparrow fable / dedication

Lines 1–85. OUP copyright and catalog data (ISBN 978-0-19-967811-2). The Unfinished Fable of the Sparrows: a flock seeks an owl helper before solving owl-taming; Scronkfinkle warns about the control problem; the book is dedicated to him and those who work on control before capability.

Preface

Lines 86–209. Human cognitive advantage compounded into dominance; if machine brains exceed ours, our fate depends on their actions and on solving the control problem in the one window when we still build the system. Scope: pathways and timing in early chapters; bulk on post-breakthrough kinetics, forms, strategic advantage, and control; closing strategic recommendations. Epistemic modesty, target audience, and endnote depth. Acknowledgments to FHI collaborators and a wide AI-risk network (Russell, Hassabis, Musk, Yudkowsky, Ord, and others).

Contents / lists of figures, tables, and boxes

Lines 210–444. Full table of contents with chapter subheads; lists of figures, tables, and boxed inserts that structure the technical argument.

Ch. 1 — Past developments and present capabilities

Lines 445–1426. Growth modes and “big history” (Hanson-style doubling times; hypothetical post-industrial growth); intelligence explosion distinguished from vague “singularity” talk; recurring twenty-year AI forecasts; I. J. Good’s 1965 intelligence-explosion passage; Dartmouth 1956 origins, seasons of hope and despair, and AI winters; survey of then-current capabilities; structured review of expert opinion on machine-intelligence timelines.

Ch. 2 — Paths to superintelligence

Lines 1427–2779. Five routes surveyed without offering an AGI blueprint: artificial intelligence (learning-centric design, child-machine and evolutionary arguments); whole brain emulation; biological cognition enhancement; brain–computer interfaces; networks and organizations. Feasibility, timelines, and comparative advantages of each path; summary comparison.

Ch. 3 — Forms of superintelligence

Lines 2780–3231. Taxonomy: speed superintelligence, collective superintelligence, and quality superintelligence; direct versus indirect reach of digital minds; sources of structural advantage for digital intelligence (speed, duplicability, etc.).

Ch. 4 — The kinetics of an intelligence explosion

Lines 3232–3982. Slow, fast, and moderate takeoff scenarios and what each implies for human reaction time; recalcitrance along non-machine, emulation, and AI paths; optimization power and conditions for explosive versus gradual escalation.

Ch. 5 — Decisive strategic advantage

Lines 3983–4521. Whether a frontrunner project would obtain decisive strategic advantage (DSA); project scale, monitoring, and international collaboration; historical technology-race analogies; transition from DSA to a singleton shaping global outcomes.

Ch. 6 — Cognitive superpowers

Lines 4522–5176. Strategically relevant “superpowers” (intelligence amplification, strategizing, social manipulation, hacking, technology research, economic productivity); a stepwise AI takeover scenario; scope of power over nature and other agents once superintelligence is loose.

Ch. 7 — The superintelligent will

Lines 5177–5626. Orthogonality thesis (intelligence and final goals vary independently); instrumental convergence thesis (self-preservation, resource acquisition, goal-content integrity, etc.); routes to predicting motivation—design, inheritance from uploads, and convergent instrumental reasons; paperclip-style final goals as technically easy cases.

Ch. 8 — Is the default outcome doom?

Lines 5627–6170. Argument that absent deliberate alignment work, first superintelligence plus orthogonality plus instrumental convergence yields existential catastrophe as a plausible default; the treacherous turn (cooperative weak AI, dangerous strong AI); perverse instantiation and malignant failure modes; social and industry pressures that could push deployment anyway.

Ch. 9 — The control problem

Lines 6171–6980. Two agency problems (sponsor–developer; project–superintelligence); capability control (boxing, stunting, tripwires, motivation selection overview) versus motivation selection (direct specification, domesticity, augmentation, induction, coherence); incentive methods; limits of observation-based safety after the treacherous turn; brief anthropic-capture aside.

Ch. 10 — Oracles, genies, sovereigns, tools

Lines 6981–7562. Four “castes” of superintelligent system and how each maps to control strategies; oracles as question-answers with boxing and domesticity; genies, sovereigns, and tool-AIs; mutual mimickability among castes; preview/confirmation mechanisms and their limits.

Ch. 11 — Multipolar scenarios

Lines 7563–8755. Contrast with singleton/unipolar outcomes; economic multipolar models (wages, unemployment, horses-vs-humans analogy, Malthusian dynamics); protection of property rights and copyable digital labor; evolutionary and game-theoretic competition among superintelligent agencies; possible later coalescence into a singleton.

Ch. 12 — Acquiring values

Lines 8756–9870. The value-loading problem—specifying human-meaningful final goals in code before capability outruns correction; utility functions and the difficulty of defining “happiness” down to machine primitives; associative value accretion and imprinting analogies; evolution-based and learning-based value acquisition; goal-content integrity as obstacle.

Ch. 13 — Choosing the criteria for choosing

Lines 9871–10743. Indirect normativity to avoid locking in present moral error; Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV) and Yudkowsky’s desiderata (moral growth, avoiding hijacking humanity’s destiny, reducing fight-over-seed-AI incentives); evaluation of CEV’s promise and limits; alternative normative proposals.

Ch. 14 — The strategic picture

Lines 10744–11999. Person-affecting versus impersonal standpoints; differential technological development and the technological completion conjecture; state risks versus step risks; whether to accelerate or decelerate macro-structural change given existential risk; science-and-technology strategy applied to machine intelligence.

Ch. 15 — Crunch time

Lines 12000–12243. “Philosophy with a deadline”—deferring some eternal questions to post-superintelligence successors; identifying urgent, positive-value problems before the intelligence explosion; capability control as temporary; closing image of many children with independent detonators and no adult supervisor; reduction of existential risk as overriding impersonal priority.

Notes

Lines 12244–14801. Chapter-keyed endnotes with citations, technical elaboration, and qualification of main-text claims.

Bibliography

Lines 14802–~15880. Full reference list for works cited in text and notes.

Index

Remainder through ~16120. Subject index to concepts, names, and scenarios.

Coverage Guide

Intelligence explosion framing (core vocabulary)

Chapters 1 and 4 for growth-mode context, Good/Vinge/Kurzweil lineage, and takeoff kinetics. Primary mining ground for “singularity” vs intelligence-explosion distinctions in wiki glossaries.

Paths and timelines to superintelligence

Chapter 2 for AI, emulation, biology, BCI, and organizational routes; Chapter 1 for historical AI seasons and expert surveys. Use for technology-roadmap and forecasting pages—not as US policy chronology.

Strategic advantage and singleton dynamics

Chapters 5–6 for DSA, singleton formation, cognitive superpowers, and takeover scenarios. Relevant to great-power competition and tech-race analogies within this wiki’s strategic discourse.

Motivation theory (orthogonality / instrumental convergence)

Chapter 7 for goal independence and convergent subgoals; Chapter 8 for default-outcome and treacherous-turn arguments. Central to AI-risk concept pages.

Control problem and system castes

Chapters 9–10 for capability vs motivation control, boxing, oracles/genies/sovereigns/tools. Use for safety-governance and containment debates.

Multipolar economics and social outcomes

Chapter 11 for Hanson-style multipolar models, wages/unemployment, horses-vs-humans, Malthusian rebound. Bridge to labor, inequality, and automation discourse—not Bostrom’s main US narrative.

Value loading and indirect normativity

Chapters 12–13 for specification difficulty, CEV, and avoiding moral lock-in. Core for alignment and “what should the AI want?” pages.

Long-run technology strategy

Chapter 14 for differential development, state/step risk, acceleration vs deceleration; Chapter 15 for actionable priorities and existential-risk reduction as overriding goal.

Silicon Valley / AI-culture bridge (thin, optional)

Preface acknowledgments, Chapter 1 expert-opinion material, and industry-momentum arguments in Chapter 8 for post–Cold War AI-community context only within this wiki.
