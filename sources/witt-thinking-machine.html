Overview

The Thinking Machine is Witt’s reported biography of Jensen Huang and Nvidia: how a niche maker of PC graphics accelerators became the hardware backbone of the modern AI boom. The book opens on a 2023 Denny’s breakfast interview (Huang tip $1,000; seven plates), then tracks Huang from Taiwan/Thailand immigration and Appalachian boarding-school trauma through AMD and LSI Logic, the 1993 cofounding with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, the near-death NV1 pivot, parallel GPUs for Quake, CUDA as a decade-long Wall Street–defying bet, Toronto’s AlexNet on consumer GeForce cards, the post-2012 all-in on deep learning, transformers and ChatGPT-era hyperscale, and Huang’s near-monopoly on AI training chips as Nvidia’s market value crossed into the trillions. Witt draws on nearly two hundred interviews; Huang cooperates with access but refuses to read the prior New Yorker profile and hopes to “die before” the biography appears.

For this United States 1990–present wiki the source is central to post–Cold War Silicon Valley hardware, the PC gaming industry, fabless semiconductor design with TSMC, private AI infrastructure and cloud hyperscalers, OpenAI/ChatGPT as industrial demand shock, and the public mythology of founder-led chip companies. EPUB structure: Dedication; Epigraph (Marcus Aurelius); Introduction; Part I (Chs. 1–11); Part II (Chs. 12–23); Acknowledgments; About the Author; endnotes; copyright. Brief portions earlier appeared in The New Yorker (2023).

Introduction

Lines 127–310. Thesis: niche video-game hardware vendor becomes most valuable company; Huang’s thirty-two-year S&P 500 CEO tenure; parallel-computing bet against a “zero percent” historical success rate; 2012 Toronto neural-net breakthrough on consumer cards; Nvidia’s shift from ~200gamingaccessoriestomultimillion−dollarAIsupercomputerspoweringMidjourney,ChatGPT,Copilot.Denny’sbreakfastsetpiece(Huangasformerdishwasher/busboy;“You’realwaysanimmigrant”);Brannan/GoldRushshovelanalogyfor30,000 AI chips; Huang dismisses doomer AI fears (“horses can’t type”; fiction writers first); reporting method (~200 interviews; Huang elusive, self-contradictory, motivated by fear of letting employees down). Closes by pointing back to the 1973 Kentucky arrival.

Part I / Ch. 1 — The Bridge

Lines 313–679. Ten-year-old Jensen arrives Oneida, Kentucky, 1973 with brother Jeff while parents remain in Thailand; rickety footbridge bullying at Oneida Elementary; racial slurs; friendship with Ben Bays; Bruce Lee bluff and determination. Family: Taipei birth February 1963; Bangkok refinery years; mother’s dictionary-word English drills; parents’ plan for U.S. education. Oneida Baptist Institute / cleaning toilets / table tennis; reunification in Oregon; Beaverton high school; Oregon State electrical engineering; lab-partner romance with Lori Mills via homework “superpower”; breadboards to semiconductors; graduation into Silicon Valley chip boom.

Ch. 2 — Large-Scale Integration

Lines 680–1137. Christmas 1984 AMD Moscone party proposal to Lori; overnight drive; black-ice Supra crash near California–Oregon border; neck brace. AMD youth hire ($28,700 starting salary); move to LSI Logic under Wilf Corrigan culture; chip design craft and “large-scale integration”; Stanford master’s while working; friendship with Jens Horstmann; ambition to “run something by the age of thirty”; meeting future cofounders Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem (Sun Microsystems GX graphics; Priem’s intensity); Sega/3D opportunity forming as SGI and Sun ignore consumer PC gaming.

Ch. 3 — New Venture

Lines 1138–1547. 1993 Denny’s San Jose back booth with Malachowsky and Priem; $50M revenue threshold spreadsheet; Jim Gaither incorporation; name “Nvidia” (invidia / envy); Sequoia and early VC; NV1 architecture with Priem’s quadratic mapping vs industry triangles; Sega deal and Diamond Edge boards; Virtua Fighter bundle spike then return-wave failure (no depth buffer; Windows crashes); Christmas-party hubris before 1996 insolvency; Huang documents fab day on VHS; crowded field (3dfx, ATI, S3, Matrox).

Ch. 4 — Thirty Days

Lines 1548–1826. David Kirk’s 1996 arrival: skeleton staff of ~35 after mass layoff; weekly paper checks; hardware emulator in the break room replacing ping-pong table; skip-prototyping gamble to mass-produce a Microsoft-compatible knockoff; Priem vs Huang shouting matches over quadratic mapping as proxy for control; Kirk curbs Priem’s ambitions; NV3 / Riva 128 ships as 128-bit copycat with Swiss-Army features; first smooth 30 fps illusion; company clawback from brink.

Ch. 5 — Going Parallel

Lines 1827–2286. Final day-long Huang–Priem blowup (1998); Apple-mediator comparison (“significantly worse”); Priem demoted to patents, keeps shares; Kirk as consigliere and competitor-brain poacher (Riva demo kills rival firm). Parallel shaders for Carmack’s Quake: twin pipelines on one chip vs 3dfx’s multi-chip cards; Huang converts after first-principles study of Cray/nCube failures; TSMC relationship (Morris Chang; early chemical-process mishap then strategic marriage); Taiwan night-market nostalgia; TNT branding via Dan Vivoli; Carmack endorsement makes Nvidia fetish hardware.

Ch. 6 — Jellyfish

Lines 2287–2568. 1997 Dallas backgammon: Nack Ballard vs Malcolm Davis’s neural-net program Jellyfish—framed as truer dawn of machine age than Kasparov–Deep Blue. History of neural nets from 1940s “nervous nets” through Minsky’s 1969 winter; symbolic AI and expert-systems busts; Gerald Tesauro’s TD-Gammon; Fredrik Dahl’s poker/slots neural work and no-limit hold’em wall. Sets intellectual backdrop for later GPU–neural symbiosis while mainstream AI still scorns the approach.

Ch. 7 — Deathmatch

Lines 2569–3089. Pro gamer Johnathan “fatal1ty” Wendel: 140 ms twitch shots; TNT2 frame-rate edge as Nike-to-Jordan sponsorship analogy. Nvidia’s six-month ship cadence; GeForce (NV10) vs 3dfx Voodoo; patent war, countersuits, and 3dfx’s collapse into asset sale; internal 3dfx view of Huang as Darth Vader; Kirk’s hire list of 120 engineers; “mutate, grow, expand” culture clash with 3dfx play-hard alumni. ATI/Radeon emerges as lasting duopoly rival; programmable shaders and Xbox 2001 deal set up next crisis.

Ch. 8 — The Compulsion Loop

Lines 3090–3410. 2002 table-tennis thwock montage: dot-com crash, leaf-blower GeForce fan, Xbox arbitration collapse, SEC accounting probe; reunion with Joe Romanosky; teppan-grill home life vs Darth Vader office persona. Stock down >90%; Christine Hoberg CFO exit; restatement shows more profit (Coxe: “fishing expedition”). Xbox edge-detection IP fight; Microsoft walks next-gen GPU. Gaming “compulsion loop” / addiction foreshadow; APA “internet gaming disorder” (2013) as coda on the market Nvidia helped build.

Ch. 9 — CUDA

Lines 3411–4010. Ian Buck’s 32× GeForce Quake wall at Stanford → Brook language (DARPA) smuggling science into triangle hardware; Huang hires Buck (2004). SGI decline; Bill Dally courtship begins. John Nickolls invents CUDA—“video game card on every scientist’s desk”; free 2006 launch; “CUDA tax” vs Radeon; gamers subsidize R&D; mammogram alpha tests; Wall Street hostility; Nickolls’s dying insistence CUDA will change the world. Debi Shoquist packaging expedite (“That’s the right answer”); CUDA as platform lock-in seeking a killer app.

Ch. 10 — Resonance

Lines 4011–4434. Bill Dally portrait: patents, plane ditching in Long Island Sound, MIT Jellybean parallel machine, Stanford chair; turns down Intel to join money-losing Nvidia (2009) as chief scientist—“Intel is going to crush Nvidia.” Moore’s Law leakage; Jeff Smith / Starboard Value raid (Olive Garden playbook); Huang doubles down on CUDA despite “money pit” perception; Icera mobile misstep as raid trigger. Thesis: ship CUDA on every retail card to find the “lunatic” whose neglected science needs the firepower—resonance between architecture and unknown workload.

Ch. 11 — AlexNet

Lines 4435–4845. Geoffrey Hinton’s Toronto outcasts; Alex Krizhevsky (best programmer Hinton met) and Ilya Sutskever; neural nets as ideological pariah; “machine learning” rebrand. Two GeForce GTX 580s in a bedroom; ImageNet; SuperVision / AlexNet crushes 2012 contest (~85% vs ~70% prior SOTA); Fei-Fei Li’s disbelief (Honda Civic land-speed analogy); Google replication (WojNet) and talent war; Hinton: without CUDA, too much trouble. Closes Part I: two failed underdog technologies finally pay off together.

Part II / Ch. 12 — O.I.A.L.O.

Lines 4848–5223. Bryan Catanzaro (Russian lit + circuits; Siberia mission; Intel Larrabee intern who saw Moore’s wall) becomes AI-from-below prophet; parking-lot draft for cuDNN team. Huang’s Friday email: no longer a graphics company—by Monday an AI company. Whiteboard acronym O.I.A.L.O. = “Once in a Lifetime Opportunity.” Matrix-multiply / fuzzy weights / inference vs training; Philippe Vandermersch as “Wozniak” to Catanzaro’s Jobs; GTC 2014 first public AI association; Starboard doubles money and vanishes.

Ch. 13 — Superintelligence

Lines 5224–5653. Post-fifty Huang abandons hobbies, movies, teppan; only AI. Stock finally clears 2001 S&P peak; cloud hyperscalers (Google Mack Truck GPU buys; Amazon, Microsoft, Oracle) build rack-scale GPU utilities. DGX / Pascal P100: compute faster than data can arrive; “computer” transcends arithmetic bottleneck. Internal culture of speed-of-light schedules and zero-billion-dollar markets; Huang’s fifty-plus direct reports and no successor.

Ch. 14 — The Good Year

Lines 5654–6150. 2016–17 glory: Nvidia +224% then AMD +309% under cousin Lisa Su (first cousin once removed; tiger-parent New York path); private trash-talk vs public courtesy; 2006 AMD merger talks die when Huang won’t give up CEO chair. Product blizzard; Isaac → Omniverse simulation stack; reality-simulator as classic Huang zero-billion-dollar bet; GeForce/Radeon share wars.

Ch. 15 — The Transformer

Lines 6151–6546. Jakob Uszkoreit at Google: self-attention, tokens, design-for-GPU vs recurrent nets; “Attention Is All You Need” lineage; Sutskever watches two leaps (AlexNet, transformer) and hunts the next. Large language models; Huang orders Bas Aarts to scrap RNN compiler tooling for transformer stack; OpenAI / Microsoft scale as demand signal.

Ch. 16 — Hyperscale

Lines 6547–6810. Huang’s scale gospel: AI as software on sold hardware, not mystical superintelligence; GTC “supercharged law” (AlexNet week → DGX-2 eighteen minutes). Mellanox / Infiniband acquisition (~$7B, 2019–20) after Starboard raids Waldman—data-center nervous system; “Nvidia Israel”; Stevens: one of recent memory’s most profitable deals. Networking as synergy that turns GPU piles into one chip.

Ch. 17 — Money

Lines 6811–7087. GPT-3 training-data controversies (Wikipedia, Times archives, suspected Books2/LibGen); ChatGPT Nov 2022 viral launch; GPT-4 exam scores and napkin-to-website demos; Witt’s job anxiety. Equity windfalls for early Nvidia hands (Kirk’s “more money than I ever thought”); opaque Priem fortune; Microsoft’s $10B+ OpenAI stake; AI as spiritual first-use experience (Sutskever).

Ch. 18 — Spaceships

Lines 7088–7326. Voyager campus tour: black “mountain,” vertical gardens, AI janitor dispatch cameras; employees beatified as market cap hits ~$3T; QC labs as dwarves in the mountain. Ray tracing / photoreal ramen shop; “Diane” avatar blackheads; Huang’s goal to unify graphics and generative AI into inhabitable worlds. Catanzaro’s anti–sci-fi close: AI won’t play zero-sum with humans.

Ch. 19 — Power

Lines 7327–7517. Dominion Energy live-line work in Loudoun County, Virginia—data-center capital; inference (not only training) drives load; Google search energy 10× with generative AI; nuclear and coal revival talk vs climate-modeling irony. James Earl Jones voice clone; radiologist/farm-robot labor displacement; Elliott Management 2024 bubble warning on tech/Nvidia; vaporware enterprise AI.

Ch. 20 — The Most Important Stock on Earth

Lines 7518–7955. Feb 21–22, 2024 earnings Judgment Day: ~60Brevenue,>7030B net (~prior thirty years combined); $277B single-day market-cap gain; Goldman’s “most important stock on earth.” Huang as Edison of AI factories; fifty-five direct reports, no successor; Glassdoor #1 employer; “life’s work” career framing; two fringe fields (parallel computing + neural nets) fused toward sentience.

Ch. 21 — Jensen

Lines 7956–8220. Cult of mononym “Jensen”; leather-jacket visionary slot after Jobs; struggle sessions and catchphrase catechism (“speed of light,” “zero-billion-dollar market”); self-contradiction as real-time thinking; Hao Ko wardrobe roast; $100B+ “Do you know how rich I am?” not as boast but unsettled inventory. Leadership: chaotic, dictatorial, never boring.

Ch. 22 — The Fear

Lines 8221–8603. Yoshua Bengio’s 2023 Montreal “conversion” walk after ChatGPT; extinction fear worse than nuclear (designer pathogen); Hinton quits Google; Sutskever on alignment—top-cited trio alarmed. Yann LeCun as skeptic foil. Contrast with Huang’s unshaken optimism; industry open letters Huang won’t sign; doomerism among the Toronto lineage that first proved GPUs for deep learning.

Ch. 23 — The Thinking Machine

Lines 8604–8833. Post–GTC 2024 final interview: whiteboard office wrapped in stack diagrams; Oppenheimer / FTC / French raid pressure. Arthur C. Clarke clip detonates Huang—rage against sci-fi framing, job-destruction questions, therapy-style biography; “serious people doing serious work.” Eos 10,000-chip supercomputer tour (GPT-3 trained in <4 minutes); Witt’s coda: executives fear Jensen’s yell more than human extinction; American Daedalus seeing atom-to-consciousness stack; the thinking machine getting smarter with each fan spin.
